# Applying MOSAIC derivatives to problems and conjectures

## Purpose

This note records a worked application of the exponent-shift calculus and a
repeatable workflow for moving from a mathematical question to a checked
claim. It distinguishes analytic calculations from the Lean theorems that
have actually been kernel-checked.

## Worked problem: classify the stationary geometry of

\[
G(x,t)=x^{x+t},\qquad x>0.
\]

The first differential is

\[
dG=G\left(\frac{x+t}{x}+\ln x\right)dx
  +G\ln x\,dt.
\]

Since \(G>0\), a stationary point must satisfy

\[
\ln x=0,\qquad \frac{x+t}{x}+\ln x=0.
\]

The first equation gives \(x=1\); the second then gives \(t=-1\).
Thus the only stationary point is \((1,-1)\), with \(G(1,-1)=1\).

Writing \(L=(x+t)/x+\ln x\), direct differentiation gives

\[
G_{xx}=G\left(L^2+\frac{x-t}{x^2}\right),\qquad
G_{xt}=G_{tx}=G\left(L\ln x+\frac1x\right),\qquad
G_{tt}=G(\ln x)^2.
\]

At the stationary point the Hessian is

\[
H(1,-1)=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H(1,-1)=-1.
\]

The negative determinant proves that the stationary point is a nondegenerate
saddle. In particular, the tempting conjecture that the stationary point is
a local extremum is REFUTED by the Hessian test.

## A reusable conjecture workflow

For each proposed identity, inequality, monotonicity claim, or optimization
statement:

1. Type the claim. Record variables, domains, quantifiers, assumptions,
   and whether the claim is exact, asymptotic, or numerical.
2. Generate independent views. Derive symbolically, inspect limiting
   cases, evaluate representative points, and search for counterexamples.
   Numerical agreement is evidence for exploration, never a proof.
3. Attack the conjecture. Test boundaries, singularities, sign changes,
   degenerate parameters, symmetry, scaling, and small integer/rational
   cases. Preserve counterexamples rather than averaging them away.
4. Decompose the proof. Route the claim into definitions, algebraic
   identities, derivative obligations, inequalities, compactness or
   convergence obligations, and domain side conditions.
5. Verify each obligation at its proper level. Use exact arithmetic for
   finite computations, independently checked certificates where available,
   and the Lean kernel for formal theorems. A proof of a derivative formula
   does not automatically prove a global optimization claim.
6. Record the result and its scope. Use CONJECTURE, REFUTED, CONDITIONAL,
   EXPERIMENTAL, PROVED, or FORMALLY_VERIFIED accurately. Bind every result
   to the exact statement, assumptions, checker, and source revision.

## Candidate conjectures generated by the worked example

These are next research targets, not established theorems:

1. General affine exponent family. For
   F(a,b,c)(x,t)=x^(a*x+b*t+c) on x>0, characterize all stationary
   points and classify them as extrema, saddles, or degenerate points as
   parameters vary.
2. Curvature phase diagram. Determine the exact regions in (x,t)-space
   where the Hessian is positive definite, negative definite, indefinite,
   or singular. The determinant and principal minors give a concrete
   route; sign charts and boundary cases must be checked.
3. Discrete-to-continuous correspondence. For fixed positive base,
   compare finite shifts F(t+h)-F(t) with the derivative as h→0,
   including explicit remainder bounds rather than only convergence of
   secant slopes.
4. Generalized shift spectra. For the sequence a_k=q^k*a_0, study
   finite-difference eigen-relations and their stability under perturbations
   of the recurrence. The exact relation Δ^r a_k=(q-1)^r*a_k is a proved
   starting point; perturbation bounds are a separate obligation.
5. Optimization under constraints. For level sets or constraints such
   as x+t=c, derive the restricted one-variable problem and compare its
   stationary points with the unconstrained saddle. Constraint qualification
   and boundary behavior must be explicit.

## What this application establishes

The exponent-shift family is now more than a differentiation example: it
provides a compact test bed for conjecture generation, counterexample search,
stationary-point analysis, curvature classification, and formal proof
decomposition. The current formal backend still has a narrower supported
grammar than the full mathematics above. In particular, the polynomial
derivative checker described in
[Lean polynomial derivatives](LEAN_POLYNOMIAL_DERIVATIVES.md) does not
currently accept arbitrary real powers or multivariate derivatives; those
claims require the dedicated Lean development in
formal/polynomial/Main.lean and successful CI.
